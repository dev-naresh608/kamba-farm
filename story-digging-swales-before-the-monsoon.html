Digging Swales Before the Monsoon

How a few shallow trenches along the contour now hold rain on the hillside instead of letting it run off.

water_dropSoil & water
07 Oct 2026·7 min read·By Michael

When we first arrived, every heavy rain carried a layer of topsoil down the slope. The fix was not a dam or a tank. It was a set of shallow trenches, called swales, dug exactly along the contour lines of the hill.

Because a swale is perfectly level, rainwater fills it evenly and soaks slowly into the ground instead of rushing downhill. The soil below each trench stays moist for weeks after the rain stops.

“We stopped asking how to bring water to the farm, and started asking how to keep the water that already falls on it.”

We plant the mound on the downhill side of every swale with vetiver, fruit trees and nitrogen-fixing shrubs. Their roots hold the earth together and drink the stored water through the dry months.

Each year in May, before the first rains, the whole team spends a few days clearing silt from the swales and repairing the mounds. It is hard work, and it is the most valuable week on our calendar.
